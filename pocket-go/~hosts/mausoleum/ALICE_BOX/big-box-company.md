# {{title}}

### the dream

Documentation industrial software. Fluorescent lights. Beige carpet. An office that ships tools for brains that arrive as fragments and still need a section heading. Not Charlie's Toys. Not DATBOX. Office / Confluence energy without the cloud romance — product briefs, section bins, ship dates that slip by one quarter forever.

The Alice Box line was supposed to be readable desks you could launch from Deck Host: a production strut, a request bay, a documenter. Specialized house formats. Thin ROMs. Honest storage. No main-character chrome.

Great Road Mapper (CO.BBC-001-GRM) was the personal production board — phases, gates, people, reverse-calc from ship — so you could think without a Confluence paste wall. ReqRep (CO.BBC-002-RR) was the request bay — discuss in blocks, seal what is true, lock purpose, sign Product prep before anyone builds. sopr Documenter sat quieter on the line. BugBox waited in the catalog for a landing that never quite arrived.

### It never was.

The company stayed small on purpose and also stuck. Greg kept the real dates. Great Road Mapper stayed personal, not a company rollout. ReqRep shipped a first slice and then lived as a desk without a product shell — folder facts had nowhere to sit except the Big Box Co campus or every leaf at once. Product docs got written as agent shorthand before they got written as help. The marketeers shelf filled with planning notes while the product binders stayed thin.

There was a with-carl drawer of weeklies and product laws, then a product-archive of QA tombs. PDF import broke and had to be rebuilt through mint. The Firefly mock was genre, not wireframe law. The boring company was real; the clean distributable manuals and the folder-level catalog were always one shell design away.

### what still gifts

A house rule: boring company, honest storage. SKUs with ports and ROM ids. A products bay under `go.bigboxco/products` with startup pages and a docs binder that can hold real help. The Documentorias as the documenter's shelf. The lesson that unsigned talk is not law — signed prep is — even when the bay is unfinished.

The desks still run. The clay is still on the floor.
