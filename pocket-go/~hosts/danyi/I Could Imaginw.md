# I Could Imagine

2001 Accessing: I Could Imagine
I could imagine.
I could imagine the smell in the air, it smelled of tar and loathing and creation. I could feel it, cold like ice, and yet soothing. It was the feeling of a numb over taking me, and I could help but resent my choices up until now. More so then that, I resented the feeling of nothingness that now became the distant hum of the morning, the waking cry of the night. I was lonely, and that begged in my eyes like a hungry wolf, obviously not there to socialize. It was all a craving, a hopeless temptation -- this was seriously the work of the devil.

Before me lay the cumbersome walk across the mall's parking lot alone, and for what purpose? It was one of those days where the city air smelled so sickeningly sweet that you felt inspired, or perhaps driven by something, to go outside and somehow interact with your peers. I chose the mall. The small one. The one no one ever went to.

Hopefully, it wouldn't smell like tar in there.

It was cool inside. Cooler inside then the heated outside air that set just before the come of fall. It sat heavy on your skin, like moisture just waiting to collect. It now smelled not of tar, but of elderly ladies perfume, which made my head pound after walking with the sun shining brightly in my eyes. They now slowly readjusted to the dimmer lights.

Typical smell of a mall, I thought. A typical fucking mall.

The noise was a distant hum of crappy elevator music, hard to discern fully over the chattering whispers they called talking, coming from the food court. Mostly elderly folks came here. You know, the ones who got their rocks off going to the underclass mall for dinner at the Chinese place. 
As an observation, I personally think the Chinese place needs to not overcook their fried rice.

Not to mention the noodles are hard.
