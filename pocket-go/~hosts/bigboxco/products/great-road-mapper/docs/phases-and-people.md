# Phases, gates, and people

## Where dates come from

Unless you are hand-editing, phase and gate dates on a title usually come from a **configuration** applied at create (or when ship is set). See {{link:crate.B160B0C0E0004B26|Configurations}} for reverse-from-ship, templates, and variations.

A common full-production spine looks like:

Design → Static Art → FX → Deliver Math → Deliver Assets → Dev → Deliver Audio → QA Loc → QA LQA → QA RQA → Active Rollout → Exclusivity → Global Release

That list is an example house shape, not a hard-coded religion. Your templates decide the bins.

## Phases vs gates

Phases are ranges of work. Gates are point deadlines / handoffs. Keeping them separate is part of the cognitive cleanup — a delivery day is not the same kind of object as a craft bin.

Workstreams can sit under a phase (full phase or a portion window). People can pin to a whole phase or to a workstream slot.

## Change trail

Editing a phase or gate asks for a **reason**. Schedule moves should leave a trail, not silent date nudges.

When a mid-spine phase needs more time, the desk prefers growing **backward** (earlier lines shift earlier) while **ship stays put**. That matches the configuration rule: release is the anchor.

Edits happen in-desk (no browser prompt/confirm).

## People

1. Maintain the **roster** on the People view.
2. From a title, **Assign** pins a person to a phase or workstream with a role on that slot.
3. Assignments show as crew on cards and in the drawer.

## Linked titles (optional pattern)

Titles can point at each other when two schedule machines belong to one creative story — for example a full production config and a thinner follow-on config. How you name those machines is your house’s business. Great Road Mapper only needs the link and the configurations behind them.
