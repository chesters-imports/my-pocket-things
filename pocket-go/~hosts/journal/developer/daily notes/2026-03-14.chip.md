**Key Principles of Quantum Mechanics:**

- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave%E2%80%93particle_duality|Wave-Particle Duality}}:** Particles, such as electrons, exhibit characteristics of both waves and particles.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_(physics)|Quantization}}:** Energy and other properties are not continuous but come in discrete, "quantized" units (quanta).
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_superposition|Superposition}}:** A quantum system can exist in multiple states or configurations simultaneously, described by a wave function.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_in_quantum_mechanics|Probability and Measurement}}:** The outcome of a measurement is not deterministic but probabilistic; the wave function, governed by the Schrödinger equation, calculates the likelihood of finding a particle in a certain state.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle|Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle}}:** It is impossible to know both the exact position and momentum of a particle simultaneously.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_entanglement|Quantum Entanglement}}:** Two or more particles can become connected such that the quantum state of one cannot be described independently of the others, regardless of the distance separating them.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_function_collapse|Wave Function Collapse}}:** Observation or measurement causes the superposition to "collapse" into a single, definite state.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_exclusion_principle|Pauli Exclusion Principle}}:** In an atom, no two electrons can have the exact same set of quantum numbers.
- **{{outlink:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_tunnelling|Tunneling}}:** Particles have a non-zero probability of passing through energy barriers that classical physics would deem insurmountable.

Core Principles of Physics:

Newton's Laws of Motion:
        First Law (Inertia): Objects remain at rest or move at a constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force.
        Second Law (

    ): Acceleration is proportional to the net force applied and inversely proportional to mass.
    Third Law (Action-Reaction): For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Conservation Laws:

    Conservation of Energy: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed (e.g., potential to kinetic).
    Conservation of Momentum: Total momentum in a closed system remains constant.

Fundamental Forces and Gravity:

    Universal Gravitation: All masses attract each other, with force depending on mass and the inverse square of distance.
    Electromagnetism: Governs electric charges, magnetic fields, and light, often described by Maxwell's equations.

Thermodynamics:

    Laws of Thermodynamics: Govern heat, energy transfer, and entropy, defining how energy changes form in systems.

Modern Physics Principles:

    Relativity: Einstein's theories describe physics at high speeds and large scales (gravity as space-time curvature).
