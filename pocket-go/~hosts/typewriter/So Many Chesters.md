## Chapter 1

 
Chester's Imports was a strange
shop in the middle of downtown that hardly a soul visited. Half tech museum,
half antique landfill, it sat on the corner of South and May Ridge with its
painted moon and sun motif peeling from the beating sun. Historic, some might
say. A blight, Beverly Smith had preferred. She wrote an entire article on it,
published in the Opinions section of town's local newspaper.

Regardless of one's opinion of his
shop, most everyone would agree that Chester himself was a strange and elusive
man, one who's whole demeanor screamed secrets-well-guarded. One couldn’t be
quite sure if he was out of his mind or tapped into a different reality all
together. Mostly, everyone just assumed he really enjoyed the 1960’s,
especially the LSD.

His death came as a surprise to the
community. It was sudden and unexplained. The quiet little town bristled at the
change. A quiet little window into the good-old-days, Westlake township wasn’t
modern or changing. Westlake township liked it that way. 

So, when Samantha Rosewater, age
39, was named the heir to his strange business, the town erupted in the kind of
whispers only small towns know how to do right. Shock and wonder rippled
through the grape vines. There wasn’t a single townie who did not seem
surprised.

But no one was more surprised than
Samantha herself.

You see, Sam had just moved here a
month or three ago. From Cincinnati, 4 states away. She had come with her 2
children in tow, seeking to outrun the gauntlet of bad life decisions she had
enacted against herself thus far. And furthermore, she'd never been to this state
before. It was a random choice on a random map on a random google search rabbit
hole that maybe began with the search term: "god how do i stop ruining my
life?"

Unexpected inheritance was not on
her bingo card for those search results.

 

Over the next week, she hustled the
children onto their school buses, logged into her dull clockwork online job she
had worked for the last two decades, and tried to deny ownership to his estate.

First, she tried to ignore the
calls. When they wouldn’t stop, she opted to press the Accept button and
quickly shout, “thank-you-but-I-am-absolutely-not-interested!” into the
microphone before smashing end, never lifting the phone once to her ear.

Eventually, the lawyer stopped
calling and knocked. 

“Miss Rosewater, I presume?” said
the obviously bemused estate lawyer.

She offered him tea and a seat on
the shabby couch that had come with the rental. She excused the mess- of
herself, and of the space. She tried to brush responsibility off in any way she
could think of: She didn't know the man, she said. She didn't even know the
town six months ago. She has no family known, no business experience in that
way. Maybe another woman with the same name? Maybe it was a typo? 

She demanded to be disqualified, as
if the inheritance itself reeked of a mystery she had no desire to solve. 

Over the next hour, she chased the
estate handler through six entirely different ways to prove it. By the end of
her interrogation, you'd have sworn he looked 10 years older.

"Miss Rosewater, as I have
shown you, here- here- and here--" he said, stabbing his finger at the
lines of the will clearly outlining her name, birthdate, and social security
number, "-- that he did in fact mean you and not some *other*
Samantha Rosewater of anywhere, USA."

She sighed. “Did you check
internationally?”

“It’s your social security number,
is it not?” the lawyer groaned. His patience was waning quickly. "Listen,
will you just sign the papers already? His ex-wife is my next appointment, and
I need this whole case done. That woman makes my skin crawl."

Resigned, she signed. The lawyer
smiled.

"Congratulations, Samantha
Rosewater. You're now the proud owner of Chester's Imports. He instructed me to
leave you a welcome package on the front counter." He jingled the keys at
her, two silver and one gold, all connected to a rubber pig keychain. The eyes
popped out when squeezed. The lawyer did not hesitate to squeeze them.

No one laughed.

# Chapter 2

 

            

The interior of Chester’s Imports
made no sense. Even less so when compared to its exterior. The cosmic sun and
moon motif promised a headshop, or a music store, but the inside seemed to be
anything but. It wasn’t even clear what it was trying to be. 

She cupped her hands against the
glass, left of the additionally-out-of-place raven emblem laser-etched
permanently into the door, creating a portal of contrasted light in which she
could faintly make out the space inside. Clutter. Mis-matched towers of wood
furniture leaned and buried under boxes of old CRT monitors, free-roaming
microchip boards and cords on cords. The entrails of technology lost stacked
atop cabinets and curios with history of their own.

*Looks like how I feel,* Sam
thought. It had been two solid weeks since the lawyer’s visit and all of the
odds of life had stacked up against her. She had thrown the keys into a box
near the kitchen counter and all but forgotten she had signed her name towards
anything by the time dinner rolled around. There was unfinished work to attend
to, bills to pay, chicken nuggets to air fry. The days moved by with the dull
ache of a boring life; not meaningful yet made somehow safe in that
meaninglessness.

This morning, however? This morning
reality had bent a little different. 

The day had began as it usually
did: the blaring alarm, the struggling attempts to get a pre-teen and an
elementary student ready, each with their own unique needs and voices in the
process. The younger one was just taking ownership of her own independence and
had just requested her biggest challenge yet: full-lace sneakers. Gone were the
days of easy slip-ons on the way out the door. Now, every morning was a battle
and a lesson all wrapped up in the simple word: shoes. 

This delayed the morning, making
every moment of delay a race against the clock. The bus would come and go, with
or without them on it.

“Macy, shoes.” The girl snickered
and wiggled her toes toward her mother’s face. Sam grabbed her feet and mocked
at playfully eating them. Then, more calmly, “I am serious, we have got to go.”

Trying once again to put the
must-have-sparkle-glitter-ultra-rainbow sneakers with full-tie laces onto her
daughter’s feet, Sam regretted not enforcing or enacting some amount of
resistance in buying her such inconvenient footwear.

“Mama, oink-oink,” Macy said, her
small six-year-old body swaying, hands hidden behind her back. Her feet evaded
the sneaker entirely. Sam sighed, slowly blinked. Her brain reached to catch
the reference, to redirect the child’s focus. 

“Yes, your toes *are* little
piggies, piggies who belong in shoes,” she cooed. “What is behind your back,
Maze?”

Macy laughed. 

“Surprise!” she shouted, all feral
chaos and childhood glitter. In her hands were Chester’s keys, with that
blasted rubber pig. Macy squeezed it twice. 

“Oink! Oink!”

 

She’d tucked the kids off quickly
after that, haunted by the strange *oink* of it all. She questioned Macy about
where she’d found the keys, but the girl shrugged and pointed at her older
brother, Dexter, who also shrugged and claimed he found them weeks ago, on the
school playground. Sam frowned. Dexter had seemed to form a bit of an
exaggeration streak since they had arrived in Westlake. She had let most of it
slide. Surely, he missed his father, his friends, his life they had to leave
behind.  She didn’t want to make a big
deal out of it, he swore he was fine. 

But the signs were there. 

She opened her mouth to press on
his story when the squeal and beep of the bus echoed outside. 

“Time to go then,” she said, “We’ll
talk about this later.” She kissed each on their forehead, double-checked they
had their lunches, and off they went. She dangled the keys in front of her
eyeline – two gold, one silver. Strange, she had remembered it the opposite way.
She shoot her head to herself. Her memory was always playing tricks on her. She
had definitely spent one too many nights researching causes of early memory
loss. Maybe it was the early weed addiction, maybe it was early onset dementia.
Either way, she made an executive decision she and that pig-key ring were going
to have a little sit-down chat, whether she liked it or not. 

Now, here she was. Hands cupped
against the glass door, peering into the mystery mess like some kind of child
in a storybook realizing she had been born a princess and the castle was hers
all along. 

But instead of elation and joy, she
met it with apathy. Instead of castles and family destinies, she was gifted a
little shop of hoarders, hosted by a terrifying pig keychain mascot, and
apparently… a raven. 

“Hey little piggy,” she stated
aloud, clutching the pig and its dangling keys between index finger and thumb.
She pointed his slack, un-bulged eyes to face the raven etched into the door. “This
your buddy?” The pig did not reply. (Obviously.)

Her voice took on a bemused sense
of interrogation, turning the pig to face herself. “The quiet type, eh? Don’t
play coy! I am onto you. Look, I said. Do you know this bird?” She squeezed him
once. The pig’s eyes bulged. She laughed. *Hit a nerve, eh, *she thought
to herself.

“Scares ya, don’t it, pig?” she sneered,
holding the keychain close, meeting pig eye to eye. “Oink-oink. Who’s avoidant
now?” 

 

The silver key on the keyring was
the right one for the lock. It had an X scratched into its surface, so it was
the first one she tried. The pig never talked, and ultimately, she gave up on
her tomfoolery, and noticed the time. Her intention was to work her clockwork
job from the Imports shop today, and there was only 13 minutes before the
morning meeting was scheduled to begin. 

She scrambled inside quickly, the
cacophony of bells and chimes hung from the door startling her. She tripped
over a wire that ran directly across the front entryway, dropping the keys and the
ice latte she was holding in her free hand. The plastic cup sprang open and
coffee splattered everywhere, the ice cubes making a pleasing and yet entirely infuriating
jingle as they spread across the floor. The place was a mess, every space
possible filled with piles of junk: papers, objects, furniture, and yes: tons of
old tech gear from what appeared to be as long ago as the 70s. She couldn’t be
sure. It looked like the sci-fi future that we dreamed up in the 50s, either
way. 

Fuck. She scrambled, dropping to
her hands and knees trying to gather the liquid and ice cubes futally. She
thought about that one art installation where the robot keeps trying to keep
itself working by collecting its own oil, which it is always bleeding out from
trying to gather the oil to begin with. Her watch buzzed. A push notification.
The meeting was pushed up by 5 minutes. Fuck, fuck, fuck.

Back on her feet, she dashed to the
back. An old counter with display case in the bottom. Slam open bag, rip out
laptop. Rush to get online. Wifi seems to be named “GodsFavorites” Look all
over for wifi login. There are post-it notes all over the walls. Numbers.
Disgrams. Words. Drawings. None of them are the password. Can’t find it. Fuck. Grab
phone, put on hotspot access. Get online. 1 minute until meeting. Sweating
bullets. Green light, online, ding. Zoom call.
